Your edge VLM lives or dies on memory

1 min read

“Can we run a vision language model on the edge device?” It’s one of the most common questions I hear right now. The answer usually starts with a calculator, not a benchmark.

On most edge hardware, memory is the first wall you hit, long before compute. And the language model isn’t the only thing that needs it.

Take a device with 16 GB of shared memory. The operating system and services need some. Video decoding needs buffers for every stream. Your existing detection and tracking models need their share. Then comes the VLM. A 7 billion parameter model at 16 bit precision needs about 14 GB just for its weights. Add the KV cache, which grows with every image and every token of context, and the total doesn’t fit at all.

Quantize those weights to 4 bit, and the same model drops to roughly 4 GB. Suddenly it fits, with headroom left over.

How I would approach an edge VLM:

  • Do the full memory budget first: weights, KV cache at your real context length, video buffers, CV models and the OS, at peak load
  • Quantize, then measure accuracy again on your own data. Some tasks barely change, others degrade in ways a general benchmark won’t show
  • Don’t run it on every frame. Let classic CV handle detection, and call the VLM only on events that need reasoning
  • Keep prompts and image inputs small. Fewer image tokens means a smaller cache and faster answers
  • Plan for thermal and power limits too. A model that fits can still be too slow on a hot afternoon
