Stop ending postmortems with a name

1 min read

Early in my career, I sat through a postmortem that ended with one conclusion: an engineer ran the wrong command on a production node, and they should be more careful next time.

Three months later, a different engineer ran almost the same command on a different node. Same outage.

The first postmortem found a person. It never found the cause.

The real question was never “who did this?” It was “why was this so easy to do?” Why could one command take down a live node without a confirmation? Why did staging and production look identical in the terminal? Why didn’t monitoring catch it for twenty minutes?

Those answers lead to changes that protect everyone, not just the person in the room.

How I run postmortems now, as a staff engineer:

  • No names in the root cause. People are part of the timeline, never the conclusion
  • Keep asking why until you reach something the system could have prevented or caught
  • Every action item gets one owner and one date, and someone checks it actually shipped
  • Share the write up widely. Other teams usually have the same gap and don’t know it yet

When engineers know a postmortem won’t be used against them, they tell you what really happened. That honesty is where all the learning comes from.

If the fix is “be more careful”, you haven’t found the cause.
