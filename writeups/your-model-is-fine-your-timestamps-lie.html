Your model is fine. Your timestamps lie.

1 min read

One of the most expensive bugs I’ve seen in vision systems had nothing to do with the model.

The detections were accurate. The GPS was accurate. But when we projected objects onto the map, they kept landing a meter or more away from where they should be. And the error got worse the faster we moved.

The problem was the timestamp.

Most pipelines stamp a frame when it arrives in software. But by then the frame is already old. Light hit the sensor during exposure, then the sensor spent time on readout, then the frame sat in transfer buffers and queues. That gap is easily tens of milliseconds, and it changes with exposure time and system load.

At 72 km/h, a 60 ms error means your object is 1.2 meters away from where it really was. No amount of model tuning fixes that.

What actually fixed it:

  • Stamp every frame at the middle of its exposure, not on arrival
  • Measure the capture to arrival latency and log it per frame, because it drifts
  • Put the camera, GPS and host on one shared clock, disciplined by the GPS PPS signal
  • For rolling shutter sensors, remember each row has its own capture time
