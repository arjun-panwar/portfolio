Built to work on the worst day

1 min read

I often think about how the systems I build every day could help with problems that really matter. Flood early warning is one I keep coming back to.

The technology looks simple on paper. Point a camera at a river with a level marker, measure the water level with computer vision, and send an alert when it crosses a threshold.

The hard part is that a flood warning system has to work on the worst possible day. Heavy rain, darkness, power cuts, and a cellular network that is overloaded or completely down. That’s exactly the moment it matters most, and exactly when a cloud dependent design fails.

How I would approach it, based on what edge deployments have taught me:

  • Measure and decide on the device. The edge unit reads the level and triggers alerts locally, without waiting for a server
  • Use more than one way to raise the alarm: cellular when it works, a radio fallback, and a local siren or light that doesn’t need any network
  • Store every reading locally and send the backlog when the link returns, so the history is complete for later analysis
  • Make the reading trustworthy in bad conditions: infrared lighting for night, handling rain on the lens and glare on the water, and cross checking against a simple water level sensor
  • Monitor the camera’s own health. A dirty or blocked lens should raise its own alert, not silently report a calm river

Design for the day the storm takes out the network.
