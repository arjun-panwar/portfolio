GPS and positioning

Great satellite geometry. Wrong position.

1 min read

One of the most common mistakes I see in GPS based systems is treating HDOP as an accuracy number.

It isn’t. HDOP only describes the geometry of the satellites you are using. Satellites spread nicely across the sky give a low HDOP, and that’s good for accuracy in theory. But it says nothing about whether the signals themselves are clean.

Near tall buildings, metal structures or large vehicles, signals bounce before they reach the antenna. This is called multipath. The receiver measures a longer path than the real one and calculates a position that can be off by several meters, while still reporting plenty of satellites and a very healthy HDOP.

If your system gates on HDOP alone, that bad position passes every check and flows straight into tracking, geofencing or sensor fusion.

What I rely on instead:

  • Treat HDOP as a geometry score, never as an error bar
  • Look at the receiver’s own accuracy estimates and measurement residuals. They react to multipath where HDOP doesn’t
  • Cross check with other sensors like an IMU or wheel speed, and reject jumps that are physically impossible
  • Know your environment. Map where multipath is common and be stricter there
