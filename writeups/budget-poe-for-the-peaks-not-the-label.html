Budget PoE for the peaks, not the label

1 min read

The power budget worked out perfectly on paper. We added up the rated draw of every camera and illuminator, and the total sat comfortably under what the switch could deliver.

Then, on the first cold morning after a power cut, half the cameras kept rebooting in a loop.

Power over Ethernet ratings on datasheets usually describe normal operation. Real devices don’t stay normal. At startup, cameras draw extra power while they boot. Outdoor units switch on built in heaters when it’s cold. Infrared illuminators turn on together at dusk. And after a power cut, everything does all of this at the same moment.

When the total demand exceeds the switch budget, the switch protects itself by cutting power to some ports. Those cameras restart, draw peak power again, and the cycle repeats. In the logs, it looks like flaky cameras or a network problem.

How I plan PoE power now:

  • Budget for peak draw: startup, heaters on, illuminators on, all at once, in the coldest conditions you will face
  • Leave real headroom on top of that, because devices get added long after the original plan
  • Stagger power up after an outage, so every device doesn’t hit its peak in the same second
  • Set port priorities, so if the budget is exceeded, the least important devices lose power first
  • Monitor per port power draw, so you see the margin shrinking before a winter morning shows you
PeaksStartup and coldHeaters and IR on
HeadroomSize for worst caseNot the nominal draw
StaggerPower up in stagesSet port priorities

Size for the coldest morning, not the datasheet.
