Great detections. Terrible tracking.

1 min read

A detector can find every object in every frame and the system can still get the answer wrong.

We had exactly that. Detection quality was excellent. But dwell times were off, counts drifted, and some objects appeared to teleport across the scene. The cause was the tracker, not the detector.

Every time two objects crossed paths or one passed behind something, the tracker had to decide who was who when they reappeared. Sometimes it guessed wrong and swapped their IDs. Sometimes it gave a returning object a brand new ID. In a single frame nothing looked broken. Over time, identity was falling apart.

And almost everything the business cares about depends on identity: how long something stayed, where it went, whether it’s the same object seen earlier, how many unique objects passed.

What I focus on when a system depends on tracking:

  • Measure identity directly. Count ID switches and use identity focused metrics like IDF1 or HOTA, not just detection mAP
  • Combine motion with appearance, so the tracker can recognize an object after a crossing or occlusion instead of relying on position alone
  • Tune how long lost tracks are kept alive. Too short creates new IDs, too long causes wrong matches
  • Build test sets from the hard moments: crowded scenes, crossings, occlusions and objects leaving and returning
