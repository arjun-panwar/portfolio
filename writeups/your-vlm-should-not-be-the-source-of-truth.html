Your VLM should not be the source of truth

1 min read

Vision language models are genuinely changing video analytics. You can ask a question about a scene in plain English and get a useful answer. That’s a real shift.

But I keep seeing one design mistake: letting the VLM become the source of truth.

Ask a VLM how many vehicles are in a frame, and it will give you a confident number. Ask again with slightly different wording, and you might get a different one. It can miss an object, invent one, or describe something that happened a few frames ago. For a demo that’s fine. For counts, alarms or billing, it isn’t.

The architecture that works best in production splits the job:

  • Classic CV owns the facts. Detection, tracking, counting and zone events are deterministic, fast and testable. Those results and your database stay authoritative
  • Send the VLM structured metadata, not raw video. Events with object IDs, zones and timestamps are far cheaper to process and much harder to misread
  • Use the VLM for what it’s great at: summarizing activity, answering operator questions, explaining unusual events and turning data into readable reports
  • Never let the language layer overwrite a fact. If the VLM and the tracker disagree, the tracker wins and the disagreement gets logged
CV decidesCounts and IDsDeterministic, testable
Send metadataEvents, not videoCheaper and faster
VLM explainsSummaries, questionsNever overrides facts

The result is a system people can trust, with a natural language interface people actually enjoy using.

Let the model reason. Never let it be the record.
