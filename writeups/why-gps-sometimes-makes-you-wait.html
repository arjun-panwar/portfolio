Why GPS sometimes makes you wait

1 min read

Why does GPS sometimes find you in one second and other times make you wait half a minute?

I used to think it was just bad signal. Turns out the real reason is how slowly satellites talk.

Every GPS satellite sends out its exact orbit details, and your receiver needs that information before it can work out where you are. The catch is that this data comes in very slowly, only 50 bits per second. Hearing the full orbit details from a satellite takes around 30 seconds, and that is if nothing interrupts the signal.

That’s why there are three kinds of starts:

  • Cold start: the receiver knows nothing. It has to find satellites and listen to everything from scratch.
  • Warm start: it roughly knows the time and its last location, so the search is quicker, but it still needs fresh orbit data.
  • Hot start: it already has recent orbit data saved, so it can get a position in a second or two.
Cold start30 sec or moreStarts from zero
Warm startTens of secondsKnows time and place
Hot start1 to 2 secUses saved orbit data

The lesson for me: if your device loses its saved data every time it powers off, you’re forcing a cold start every single time. Keeping a small backup power source for the receiver’s memory or feeding it assistance data from the internet can make a huge difference.

Something so small, yet it changes how fast a device feels to the user.
