Edge hardware and security

Assume someone will touch your device

1 min read

Most security thinking assumes the attacker is somewhere far away, on the other side of the internet. At the edge, the attacker might be standing right next to your device.

Edge hardware lives in places anyone can reach: on poles, in cabinets, along roads, on moving equipment. That changes everything. Someone with a few minutes and a screwdriver can do things no remote attacker ever could.

They can unplug a camera and plug a laptop into that network port. They can pull the storage out and read it on another machine, including credentials, models and stored images. They can find a debug console that was left enabled from development.

How I think about securing devices people can physically touch:

  • Lock down the local network. Devices should prove who they are before they get access, and an unknown device on a camera port should get nothing
  • Encrypt storage, and keep the keys off the disk, ideally in dedicated secure hardware. A stolen drive should be useless
  • Close the back doors before shipping. Debug consoles, default passwords and open service ports are the first things someone will try
  • Accept only signed software updates, so nobody can load their own code onto the device
  • Give every device its own credentials, so one compromised unit doesn’t unlock the whole fleet

Camera footage is often sensitive data. Protecting it at the edge is part of building the system responsibly, not an extra.

Design as if the attacker is standing right next to it.
