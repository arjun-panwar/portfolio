Your test set is quietly cheating

1 min read

One of the easiest ways to fool yourself in computer vision is a random train and test split on video data.

It looks like good practice. You extract frames, shuffle them, put 80 percent in training and 20 percent in test. The results come back great. Then the model goes to the field and performs noticeably worse.

The problem is that neighboring frames from the same video are almost identical. Same scene, same lighting, same object, shifted by a few pixels. When you split by frame, your test set is full of near copies of images the model already trained on. You are not measuring generalization. You are measuring memory.

What I do instead:

  • Split by video or sequence, never by individual frame
  • For fleets, go further and hold out whole cameras, locations or days, so the test set looks like a deployment the model has never seen
  • Check for leakage before trusting a good number. If test accuracy is suspiciously close to training accuracy, look at where the test frames came from
  • Keep a small, fixed “hard” set from new locations that never touches training

A lower score from an honest split is worth far more than a high score you can’t trust. It’s the number that actually predicts what happens in production.
