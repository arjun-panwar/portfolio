Video pipelines and performance

Your pipeline is running on old frames

1 min read

Your model might be making perfect decisions about something that happened 165 milliseconds ago.

This one catches a lot of teams. A pipeline looks healthy in testing, accuracy is fine, GPU usage is fine, but in the field every action feels slightly late. Tracks lag behind objects. Triggers fire after the moment has passed.

The cause is usually a deep capture buffer.

Most capture APIs keep a queue of frames by default. If the camera produces 30 frames a second and your pipeline handles 25, the queue fills up and stays full. From then on you are always reading the oldest frame in the buffer, not the newest one. Five queued frames at 30 FPS means you are working about 165 ms in the past, and that number never shows up in your model metrics.

What has worked well for me in production:

  • Keep the capture buffer at one or two frames and always grab the latest
  • Drop frames on purpose when you fall behind. Skipping keeps latency flat, queueing makes it grow
  • Log the age of every frame at inference time, from capture timestamp to now, and alert when it creeps up
  • Budget latency per stage, because the model is rarely the only slow part
