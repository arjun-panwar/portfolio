That missed detection was a network problem

1 min read

We once spent days investigating why a detector kept missing objects on a few cameras. Same model, same settings, same kind of scene as every other camera. The accuracy just dropped on those streams.

The model was fine. The frames weren’t.

Video streams are compressed, and most frames only store the changes from the previous one. When the network drops a few packets, the decoder can’t rebuild part of the image correctly. You get smearing, gray blocks, or the lower half of the frame frozen from a moment ago. And that damage carries forward until the next full key frame arrives.

The model does its job on what it receives. If half the object is smeared, it misses it. From the outside, it looks exactly like a model problem.

What I check now, before anyone opens a training notebook:

  • Decoder error counts per stream. They usually rise right before accuracy drops
  • Packet loss and jitter per camera, not just averages for the whole network
  • The physical path: cable runs, PoE power budgets, overloaded switch ports
  • The transport setting. Moving a stream to a more reliable transport often fixes more than a new model would
Check firstDecoder errorsBefore the model
MeasureLoss per cameraNot per network
HardenFix the linkCables, PoE, transport

At the edge, a CV engineer ends up being half a network engineer. The earlier you accept that, the faster you debug.

Before you retrain, check that the frame even arrived whole.
