Vehicle power is not clean power

1 min read

A compute unit that ran flawlessly on the bench for weeks started rebooting randomly once it was mounted on a vehicle. A few units didn’t reboot at all. They just died.

The software was fine. The power wasn’t.

A vehicle’s electrical system is one of the harshest environments electronics can live in. The label says 12 or 24 volts, but the real line is anything but steady. When the engine starts, the voltage can dip far below nominal for a moment, which is enough to brown out a computer and reset it. When a large load switches off suddenly, the voltage can spike well above nominal. Motors, pumps and other equipment add constant electrical noise on top of all that.

A bench power supply shows none of this, which is why these problems only appear in the field.

What I treat as non negotiable for vehicle mounted compute:

  • A wide input power converter that keeps running through deep dips and tolerates spikes far above the nominal voltage
  • Electrical isolation between the vehicle’s power and the electronics, so noise and ground problems don’t travel into your sensors and compute
  • Surge and reverse polarity protection, because someone will eventually connect something wrong
  • Ignition sensing plus a small energy reserve, so the system can shut down cleanly when the vehicle turns off instead of losing power mid write
